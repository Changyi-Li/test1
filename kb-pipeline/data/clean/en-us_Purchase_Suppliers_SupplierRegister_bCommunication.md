### Communication
In this table you can add rows with phone numbers, fax numbers, cellphone numbers, and e-mail addresses for the supplier.

#### Type
Here you enter the type of communication. It can be phone, fax, e-mail, or mobile phone.

#### E-mail/Phone
Here you enter the e-mail address, phone number, fax number, or mobile phone number.
Characters that can and cannot be used in e-mail addresses
Characters that can be used:
- Lower case letters a-z, numbers (0-9), and certain special characters (!#$%&'*+/=?^_{|}~`).
- Periods (.), but not at the beginning, end, or consecutively (e.g. “test..email@example.com”).
- Standard domain format (letters, numbers, hyphens, and periods).
Characters that cannot be used:
- Non-ASCII characters (e.g. ö, ą, ñ, and é).
- Spaces (e.g. test email@example.com)
- Initial and final periods in the local part (e.g. “.test@example.com” or “test.@example.com”).
- Invalid domain formats (e.g. “test@.com”, “test@example”, or “test@example.”).

#### Remark
Here you can enter a remark or description regarding the address/number. An example of an remark here can be that the entered phone number goes to a switchboard operator.

#### Name
Here you can see/enter the name of the contact person or recipient.

#### Recipient of
Here you can decide if the e-mail address should be recipient of different types of documents. This field is inactive for the types Phone, Fax, and Mobile phone to suppliers. The following alternatives exist for e-mail to suppliers:
- Inquiry
- Purchase order
- Return order
- Delivery note (subc.)
- Order confirmation reminder
- Delivery reminder
- Claim report (case)
- Blanket order – Purchase
- Delivery schedule Silf (the Swedish association for purchase and logistics) explain the term "delivery plan" in the following way: A delivery schedule is a plan/schedule for deliveries from supplier to customer. The delivery schedule is created by customer and generally contains a planning horizon of 0,5–1 year. Normally the delivery schedule quantities are assigned different statuses depending on the type of demand. It is common that for example the entered quantities in the immediate future (closest in time) actually are fixed orders. In an interval of a few months ahead of the fixed orders, the entered quantities might be considered as preliminary orders for which the customer is obliged to take financial responsibility for any material purchased by the supplier. The subsequent quantities entered are considered to be forecast only. (Translated from source https://www.silf.se/tjanster/ordlista-for-inkop-och-logistik/l/ [2018-08-29]). A delivery schedule is a way to increase the transparency and thereby make it possible to mutually take charge of the financial situation across multiple steps in the supply chain. This is done by transferring information regarding the immediate demands/requirements as well as future forecast demands. – Purchase
