### Settings – Selection

#### Pre-select "Include"
This setting determines whether or not the “Include” box should be checked by default for all rows in the list/result.

#### Printout date
The date that is shown on the document as the date of the printout. Today's date is shown by default. By using the available calendar, you can select another date.

#### Number of copies
Here you select the number of copies of the delivery schedule that should be printed. The default value here is 1.

#### Quantity shown
Here you decide which part quantity should be shown on each row with actual purchase orders in the delivery schedule. Remaining quantity is selected by default, but you can change to Ordered quantity.

#### Use default document settings
With this setting you decide if default document settings and default variant of the document Delivery schedule Silf (the Swedish association for purchase and logistics) explain the term "delivery plan" in the following way: A delivery schedule is a plan/schedule for deliveries from supplier to customer. The delivery schedule is created by customer and generally contains a planning horizon of 0,5–1 year. Normally the delivery schedule quantities are assigned different statuses depending on the type of demand. It is common that for example the entered quantities in the immediate future (closest in time) actually are fixed orders. In an interval of a few months ahead of the fixed orders, the entered quantities might be considered as preliminary orders for which the customer is obliged to take financial responsibility for any material purchased by the supplier. The subsequent quantities entered are considered to be forecast only. (Translated from source https://www.silf.se/tjanster/ordlista-for-inkop-och-logistik/l/ [2018-08-29]). A delivery schedule is a way to increase the transparency and thereby make it possible to mutually take charge of the financial situation across multiple steps in the supply chain. This is done by transferring information regarding the immediate demands/requirements as well as future forecast demands. – Purchase should be used. If you activate this setting, any document settings saved on purchase order and settings of supplier-unique documents saved on the supplier, will be disregarded.

#### Include purchase orders
With this setting you determine whether or not the purchase orders should be included in the delivery schedule.

#### Include purchase order suggestion
Here you determine whether or not the purchase order suggestion should be included in the delivery schedule. These are displayed under the Forecast heading on the document.

#### Include purchase and manufacturing orders (subcontract)
Determines whether or not purchase and manufacturing orders of the Subcontract type will be included in the delivery schedule.

#### Include manufacturing order suggestions
Here you decide if the manufacturing order suggestions should be included in the delivery schedule. These are displayed under the Forecast heading on the document.

#### Include reported arrivals
With this setting you decide if to show reported arrivals of parts on purchase orders included in the delivery schedule. These are displayed under the Most recent deliveries heading on the document.

#### Number of arrivals
If the Include reported arrivals setting has been activated, you can decide how many arrivals should be displayed in the delivery schedule. The default value here is 3.

#### Only include parts marked as "Part in delivery schedule"
Here you decide if only parts which have the setting Part in delivery schedule activate in supplier links, will be included in the delivery schedule.

#### Include parts with no current requirement
If the above setting is activated it is also possible to activate this setting. If you activate this setting it means you will also include parts without a current requirement in the delivery schedule. In the delivery schedule, these parts will be shown with a zero quantity in the calls and the cause code is set to Normal. This is used to let the supplier know that there is no longer a need to send these parts to us, if there is still a remaining quantity left to deliver in the previous delivery schedule.

#### Our reference
In this field you select a person to use as "our reference" for the delivery schedule. You can select among the persons marked as Reference in the personnel records. The name of the person will be printed in the header of the document and disregards "our reference" on actual purchase orders in the delivery schedule.

EDI is about exchanging electronic business documents with your business partners, e.g. customers and suppliers. The EDI concept can be wide and a bit unclear, and can many times be used about all types of documents which are sent electronically, even if it might be PDF files sent via e-mail or publishing business documents on a website. What we refer to as EDI – and what is traditionally meant by EDI – is structured business documents following given standards, electronically sent or received and which are compiled and interpreted automatically and that is integrated with the customer's/supplier's ERP system. option. When this setting is activated it means the column for EDI export in the Result box by default will be set to No. This setting can be of use in order to avoid unintentional EDI export when you list the results, for example when you want to reprint a document.
